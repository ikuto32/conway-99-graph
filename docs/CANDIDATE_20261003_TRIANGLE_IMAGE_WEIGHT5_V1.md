Let G be any finite simple graph in which each edge has exactly one common
neighbor. Let T be its complete set of actual triangles, m=|T|, and let r_p
be the number of triangles through vertex p. Let B contain their binary
incidence columns. A triangle path is an unordered set of three distinct
actual triangles with intersection graph a path: one central triangle meets
both end triangles at different points, and the end triangles are disjoint.

The exact number of these paths is

  P = sum_(t in T) sum_({p,q} subset t) (r_p-1)*(r_q-1).

Their binary sums are words of weight5. Each weight5 support is the image
of at most two paths. Therefore the full code im_GF(2) B has at least
ceil(P/2) distinct weight5 words. This is a lower count, not an assertion
that every weight5 codeword is generated by a triangle path.

If every vertex has the same incidence r, then P=3m(r-1)². For any
hypothetical srg(99,14,1,2), m231,r7 give P24948 and N5>=12474. No

## The path count and its quantifiers

Distinct actual triangles share at most one point, since a shared edge
would have two common neighbors. Three actual triangles meeting pairwise
at three different points are forbidden: those points make a further
triangle using an edge whose completion is already supplied by its original
triangle. Three triangles through one common point are allowed.

Fix a central triangle t and an unordered pair of its different points p,q.
Choose any other triangle through p and any other triangle through q. The
chosen end triangles cannot meet t at another point by linearity. They
cannot coincide, since that would share the edge pq with t. They cannot
intersect each other, since such an intersection together with p,q would
be the forbidden three-different-point configuration. Thus every choice
produces a path. Conversely a path has one central triangle and one
unordered pair of intersection points, so the displayed sum counts it once.
No division for orientation or assumption of uniform secondary profiles is
hidden in the formula. Its regular version uses r only when it is uniform.

## Exact inverse and multiplicity bound

Write a path as

  {p,a,b}, {p,q,r}, {q,c,d}.

All seven displayed points are different. Its binary sum is the indicator
of S={a,b,r,c,d}. The edges ab and cd occur. The vertex r is adjacent to
none of a,b,c,d: for example, an edge ra would create triangle rpa, whereas
rp already lies in the central triangle rpq. The same argument applies to
the other three points. A cross edge between {a,b} and {c,d} can occur,
but these cross edges form a matching. For example, if a met both c and d,
the edge cd would have common neighbors a and q, which is forbidden.
The symmetric argument gives degree at most one at each cross endpoint.

Hence G[S] is precisely one of2K2+K1, P4+K1, orC4+K1. The isolated
vertex r is uniquely recoverable; the other four vertices are not isolated
because each has its original pair edge. The two end edges form a perfect
matching on those four vertices. There is one perfect matching in2K2 and
P4, and two inC4. Thus there are at most two candidates for the pair of
end edges. For each candidate matching, the unique common neighbor of each
edge reconstructs its end triangle. These determine p and q uniquely, and
the central triangle would then have to be {p,q,r}. If those data fail to
give three distinct actual triangles forming the specified path, that
matching gives no preimage; otherwise it gives exactly one unordered path.
Therefore every support has at most two preimages.

This inverse permits real two-fold collisions. In rook9 the six actual
triangles have18 unordered paths, which give nine distinct weight5 words.
Asserting path injectivity would be false. Linearity of a declared triple
system alone also does not prove the inverse: Pasch controls have additional
actual graph triangles, and a completeK7 graph has nonunique edge completions
and more than two path representations of the same five-set.
