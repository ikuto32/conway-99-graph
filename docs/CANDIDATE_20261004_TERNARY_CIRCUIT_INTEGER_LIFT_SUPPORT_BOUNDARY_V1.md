## A general necessary consequence, with no circuit-weight improvement

Let D be an integer matrix whose columns all have the same nonzero integer
coordinate sum c. Suppose a selected set of w columns is a matroid circuit
over F3, and its unique dependence generator u has sum(u) nonzero. Then those
w columns are linearly independent over Q.

Indeed the ternary column rank is w-1, so the rational rank is at least w-1:
a nonzero minor modulo three is a nonzero integer minor. If the rational rank
were w-1, its one-dimensional rational kernel would have a primitive integer
generator z. Primitivity ensures z mod3 is nonzero; it is a multiple of u,
since the ternary kernel is one-dimensional. But summing Dz=0 gives
c*sum(z)=0 over the integers, hence sum(z)=0. Its reduction would make u
balanced, a contradiction. Thus the rational rank is w.

For actual triangle columns c=3. Their selected Gram D^T D is consequently
positive definite for every unbalanced ternary circuit. Each maximal minor
of D is divisible by three, because its ternary rank is w-1; Cauchy-Binet
then makes det(D^T D) a positive integer divisible by nine. No claim of
divisibility by27 follows. These are necessary conditions, not a new upper or
lower bound on w. In particular n>=w for used-point count n was already part
of the existing circuit/Gram analysis, and is not recounted as progress.

The three-by-three matrix 3I has ternary zero columns and is outside the
circuit premise for w>1. Conversely Q=J_4-I_4 supplies a hand boundary with
w=4: modulo three it has rank3 and kernel generated by j, whose sum is4=1.
Over Q it is invertible, and Q^T Q=I+2J has determinant9. This shows that
positive determinant divisible by nine need not have another factor three.
Q is binary with column weight three, but its columns share point pairs and
it is not the complete triangle incidence of a lambda-one graph. It does not
refute the existing target lower bound on unbalanced circuit size.

## Why the attempted lifting exclusion is invalid

Every full incidence matrix has column sums three. If u is an unbalanced
right word, no integer lift U with the same residues can satisfy NU=0:
that equation would imply sum(U)=0. The same argument rules out NU=0 modulo
nine, because sum(NU)=3*sum(U). This obstruction is expected for the already
forced unbalanced right circuit; treating it as a contradiction would discard
the necessary object rather than test it.

The mod9 theorem concerns the complete99-by231 matrix. Applying its zero
left-radical assertion to the17-by12 matrix D would be a second error. Here
the rational left kernel has dimension5, and exact integer left relations
reduce to nonzero ternary left relations which certainly lift modulo nine.
The full-row-rank identity NP=210I is unavailable for D. Remaining triangle
columns in a hypothetical completion may destroy these partial dependencies;
no persistence in the complete left kernel has been proved.

For balanced words, the earlier exact global lift can introduce other
triangles and coefficients of larger magnitude. Demanding that this lift
remain on a selected circuit is therefore an additional unsupported premise.
The general real-independence lemma above and the displayed finite family do
not derive any useful circuit-weight exclusion. The old minority-sign Gram
bounds remain intact, with their exact unbalanced-circuit hypotheses.
