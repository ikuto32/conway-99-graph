# Candidate general affine phase screen for balanced parity assignments

The domain remains the exact six-prism Hadamard coordinate support, with the additional assumption that each coordinate visits all three fibres across the three columns of an identical-support group. This assumption is not without loss of generality for arbitrary factors. No automorphism is assumed. The purpose is to generalize the phase screen to every allowed normalized parity pattern, including constant groups.

Label positions and fibres by GF(3), and express each coordinate permutation as pi_i(x)=s_i*x+t_i, where s_i is 1 or -1. Relabel each group's columns by its first coordinate permutation's inverse. Then its first map is the identity, and s_i=(-1)^p_i for the normalized parity pattern. This relabeling preserves Gram, outside-column overlaps and existence of any residual completion.

Local balance says that the six permutation matrices sum to 2J. The even and odd permutation multiplicities obey a_i+b_j=2 for all nine pairs, so there are two normalized local types:

- A mixed group has three maps of each sign, with all three phases occurring once in each sign class. Each class has phase sum zero, and all phases within a sign class must differ.
- A constant group has all normalized signs positive and phase multiset {0,0,1,1,2,2}. Its six phases sum to zero. Equal phases occur and are permitted. It is incorrect to apply the mixed-group nonzero differences to a constant group.

For a coordinate pair a,b in a group, the relative map from a's fibre to b's fibre is pi_b composed with pi_a inverse. Its sign is e=s_b*s_a and its phase is u=t_b-e*t_a. The five relative permutation matrices for a nonmatched coordinate pair must sum to 2J-I. If the number of odd relative maps is zero, the five translation phases have multiplicities (1,2,2) at (0,1,2). If it is three, the three reflection phases are (0,1,2) once each, and the two translation phases are (1,2) once each. These are all possibilities.

Consequently the sum of odd relative phases is zero and the sum of even relative phases is zero in both cases. An empty odd sum is simply a zero row. These equations are necessary even when some groups are constant. Together with one gauge equation per group, one local sum for a constant group and two local sums for a mixed group, they define a homogeneous GF(3) system for 120 phases. Duplicate and zero rows should be retained with explicit metadata rather than silently changing the frozen equation population.

The first general screen should use only pairwise phase differences within each mixed group's two sign classes as required-nonzero functionals. A functional identically zero on the complete nullspace gives a conditional exclusion certificate. It does not make arbitrary row-space membership evidence sufficient: the local mixed premise, functional orientation, complete linear rows and gauge argument must all be checked. Additional pair-profile inequalities are valid but are outside the first batch's declared rejection rule unless separately added and reviewed.

For an excluded full parity assignment, the conservative valid nogood is the disjunction of the negatives of its twenty chosen pattern selectors. It excludes only that particular branch. A potentially shorter clause can be justified from a checked row-combination certificate: retain every group whose parity determines any used local row or pair row, and the group supporting the violated mixed inequality. Gauge rows have no parity dependency. A pair row conservatively depends on all five incident groups, including groups that contribute no term for that sign. Pinning this union preserves the entire certified implication. Checking only the visibly nonzero coefficients would be unsound because parity changes can alter membership in a pair's odd/even sum. No such minimized clause is produced here.

The control program exhausts all 6^6 local affine-map tuples and all 6^5 relative-map tuples. It records the exact local types, gauge orbits, both Gram profile cases and the phase-sum implications. A constant-group example with repeated equal-sign phases explicitly falsifies the stronger blanket-distinctness rule. These finite controls are local and do not construct a complete factor. The general linear theorem and future clause procedure remain candidate work pending independent review.
