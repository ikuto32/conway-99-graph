# Independent dynamic residual-screen review

Scope: a specified validated triangle core C and binary factor F. No
research-sized factor is available in this audit. The pinned residual
equivalence r1 supplies FD=H=2J-F-CF and the residual quadratic identity.
An actual completion D is symmetric binary, zero diagonal and degree eight.

Nonnegative FD requires H>=0. For y!=z, the quadratic equation gives
T[y,z]+common_D(y,z)=2-D[y,z], where T=F transpose F. Hence T[y,z]>2
excludes completion, and D[y,z]=1 requires T[y,z]<=1. That edge contributes
F[:,z] to column y of FD and F[:,y] to column z. Both directional inequalities
F[:,z]<=H[:,y] and F[:,y]<=H[:,z] are required. Their conjunction defines a
symmetric permitted-edge graph. No converse is claimed: permitted edges may
still be incompatible globally.

At a fixed residual vertex let N be the number of permitted neighbours, p
the number with a one in a fixed factor coordinate, and d the residual degree.
Every d-subset contains at most min(d,p) ones and at least
d-min(d,N-p)=max(0,d-(N-p)) ones. This follows by counting available zeros
and ones separately. If N<d there is no such subset; the explicit degree
shortage already suffices. The displayed bounds then have lower>upper,
consistently expressing an empty interval. If N>=d, every intermediate
integer is attained by choosing that many ones and the remaining zeros.
An H demand outside this interval excludes exactly the supplied factor.
Bounds for different coordinates can conflict even when all pass, so passing
the screen never establishes a residual graph or simultaneous star solution.

The producer's research path first authenticates a caller-pinned independent
factor report and requires its accepted-object status. It checks exact raw
factor bytes through either its input-hash map or its independently decoded
artifact hash, then validates the literal core, margins and Gram again.
The caller is responsible for choosing a genuine independent gate; a digest
identifies bytes and is not a signature authenticating a reviewer. No
historical matching or shift6 permutation is substituted for the raw core.

The independent checker reconstructs partial adjacency neighbour sets and
derives H and T from literal common-neighbour counts. For each prospective
Y edge it adds that edge to the two endpoint neighbour sets and checks the
affected X--Y pair caps at both ends and the Y--Y cap. It then compares every
saved permitted-edge decision, failed coordinate, quota interval and shortage.
It does not use the producer's matrix-product code to derive expectations.

Saved rook9 fixtures exercise both empty-Y triangle prerequisites and a
different nonempty generic residual split. A separately independently checked
SRG(243,22,1,2) supplies a nonempty triangle factor of size60x180 and an actual
degree16 residual graph. The producer is invoked in an isolated calibration
probe on that fixture; expectations come from the independent literal graph
checker. This is explicit black-box execution of the producer for calibration,
not shared producer code inside the independent expected-value implementation.
The larger fixture is not a Conway99 graph or factor.

Malformed gate identities, statuses, bindings, shapes, binary values, core
symmetry and margins are exercised by actual producer CLI rejection probes.
Artificial accepted-status reports used solely to reach later rejection
stages are labelled calibration-only. They never describe real accepted
research objects. Changed numerical screen arrays and quota/edge decisions
are rejected by the independent checker. Complete small quota populations
include degree larger than the available-neighbour population.

The review certifies the stated necessary bounds, source interpretation and
recorded calibrations. It does not approve an exhaustive row enumerator or
SAT encoding, assert a research factor exists, or turn an unreviewed future
screen output into a verified exclusion. Every future claimed obstruction
must bind its exact factor and raw witness to a fresh independent check.
