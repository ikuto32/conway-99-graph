# Candidate GF(3) phase equations for one all-mixed parity branch

This derivation concerns the independently checked second parity projection on the fixed six-prism Hadamard support. Every one of its twenty groups is mixed, and every nonmatched coordinate pair has three sign disagreements among its five co-occurring groups. Coordinatewise balance is an additional construction restriction; it is not assumed without loss of generality for the target.

Write the three column positions and three fibres as GF(3). Every permutation is uniquely pi_i(x)=s_i*x+t_i, with s_i in {1,-1}; its sign is determined by s_i. Relabel a group's columns by pi_0 inverse. This sets its first coordinate map to the identity. In the normalized labeling s_i=(-1)^p_i, where p_i is the stored normalized parity, and t_0=0. No automorphism of a hypothetical graph is assumed.

In a mixed balanced group the six maps are the six distinct permutations of three letters. Consequently the three phases with s_i=1 are all distinct and the three phases with s_i=-1 are all distinct. Each class therefore has phase sum zero. These two local sums, and the gauge equation, are necessary linear equations. Their accompanying distinctness conditions must not be silently discarded as a sufficiency claim.

For coordinates a,b in one group, the map relating their fibre labels is pi_b composed with pi_a inverse. Put e=s_b*s_a (since s_a inverse equals s_a). Its intercept is u=t_b-e*t_a. In particular, a parity disagreement has e=-1 and u=t_b+t_a; equal parity gives e=1 and u=t_b-t_a. This orientation is checked on every pair of affine permutations and every input letter.

For a nonmatched coordinate pair, its complete 3 by 3 Gram block equals 2J-I. Its five group contributions are permutation matrices: three reflections (e=-1) and two translations (e=1). In a mixed group, the equal-sign coordinate maps are distinct, so the translation intercepts are nonzero. The full Gram condition requires these two translations to have intercepts 1 and 2, and the three reflections to have intercepts 0,1,2. One way to derive this is the even/odd permutation multiplicity classification for 2J-I; alternatively compare its nine entries directly. The necessary linear consequences are that the three reflection intercepts sum to zero and the two translation intercepts sum to zero.

The experiment uses only these homogeneous equalities for its rank calculation. It separately records all necessary phase inequalities: local same-sign phases differ, the three reflection intercepts for a coordinate pair differ, and its two translation intercepts are nonzero. Linear feasibility alone is not a factor certificate. If a required nonzero functional belongs to the equation row space, its exact row-combination certificate would exclude this one parity branch. Any such new result needs independent verification.

When all the inequalities hold together with these equations, each coordinate-pair permutation sum is exactly 2J-I; the fixed support and balance supply the remaining Gram entries and row margins. This observation explains the possible future phase construction, but the present experiment performs no nonlinear search and supplies no outside-column caps or residual completion D.
