Let G be a finite simple graph in which every adjacent pair has exactly one
common neighbor. Let T contain every actual graph triangle once. Let r_v be
the number of triangles containing v and let B be their vertex incidence
matrix over GF(2). Define

P = sum over t in T, then unordered {u,v} subset t, of (r_u-1)(r_v-1).

An unordered triangle intersection path is three distinct triangles whose
intersection graph is a path, the central triangle meeting the two outer
triangles in different points. The separate complete path-inverse proof
audit_20261003_triangle_image_weight5_v1_proof.md establishes the following
under exactly the above premise:

* Every choice counted by P is one such path, counted once.
* Write its triangles as {u,a,b}, {u,v,r}, {v,c,d}. All seven vertices are
  distinct. Their GF(2) sum is the five-set S={r,a,b,c,d}.
* In G[S], r is the unique isolated vertex. The four other vertices induce
  2K2, P4, or C4. Each perfect matching of those four vertices determines
  at most one path preimage, by unique actual edge completions.
* Every path support has at most two preimages. Two preimages occur only
  when the nonisolated four-set induces C4, since two distinct perfect
  matchings must be present. Conversely no converse is needed here.

For completeness, the cross-edge restriction uses the adjacent-pair premise:
if a were adjacent to both c and d, edge cd would have common neighbors v
and a. Thus every vertex has at most one cross neighbor between {a,b} and
{c,d}; together with ab and cd this gives precisely the above three shapes.
For a specified matching, each matching edge has one third triangle vertex;
these fix u and v. A path can exist only if u and v are adjacent and their
unique common neighbor is r. The recovered triangles are then fixed.

Let D be the number of distinct path supports with two preimages. Map each
such support to its induced C4 on the four nonisolated vertices. This map is
injective. Indeed, fix a labelled induced C4 Q and select deterministically
the lexicographically first of its two perfect matchings, using sorted
vertex labels and sorted edge pairs. Every double-preimage support mapped
to Q must have the path using this particular matching as one of its two
preimages. The two matching-edge completions uniquely fix u and v. Their
edge completion uniquely fixes r. Therefore two different five-sets Q union
{r} cannot map to that same Q. If these completions fail to form a path,
there is simply no support mapped to Q. Existence, disjointness or adjacency
of completions is not assumed for arbitrary C4s.

Consequently D <= C4(G), where C4(G) counts induced four-vertex C4 sets.
If W is the number of distinct path supports, their fiber sizes give
P=W+D. Thus the total number N5 of weight5 words in im(B) satisfies

N5 >= W = P-D >= P-C4(G),

and together with the separate at-most-two-preimages bound,

N5 >= max(ceil(P/2), P-C4(G)).

These bounds concern the complete binary triangle incidence image; they do
not assert that every weight5 image word is generated by a triangle path.
No regularity, nonadjacent CN condition, automorphism or target existence
assumption is needed for this general implication.
